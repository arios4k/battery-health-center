Privacy and security

By Alexis Rios-Negron · Battery Health Center 0.6.5

Everything stays on your computer#

  • The app never connects to the internet and has no telemetry, no accounts and no ads.
  • Your history is a few CSV files in your home folder. Delete them whenever you like.
  • Optimized charging stores a list of unplug times on your computer. Nothing leaves it.
  • The only network activity involved is opening a link in your browser when you click one, such as this manual.

The root helper#

Changing the battery's charge limit requires administrator rights, because the firmware settings are protected. The app itself never runs as root. Instead:

  • A small program, bhc-helper, is the only part that runs as root. It is started through PolicyKit, so you approve it with your password.
  • It uses only Rust's standard library (plus libc for the local time), accepts nothing but validated arguments, and refuses anything else.
  • It writes only: the battery's charge_control_start_threshold and charge_control_end_threshold files; its own configuration files in /etc (the saved limit and the charging schedule); and a list of unplug times in /var/lib/battery-health-center for optimized charging.
  • With Keep After Restart on, a small systemd service re-applies the limit at startup and after sleep. With a schedule saved, a second service (started by a .path unit only while a schedule exists) lifts and restores the limit at the right times without asking for a password each time.

Everything else (reading battery data, drawing charts, power modes, GNOME settings) is done as you, with no extra permissions.

Open source#

The app is free software under the GPL-3.0-or-later. You can read exactly what it does in the source code. Packages are published from a signed apt repository and Fedora's COPR, so your usual updates keep it current.
